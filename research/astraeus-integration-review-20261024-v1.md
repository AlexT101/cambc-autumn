## What changed, and what survives

* `Safety::bound` returns damage, doubled-unacted damage, hits, stuns and the existing probability-weighted threat condition in one pass. Willow stations cache by `world.observations` and position; tile legality is checked at use, and their guard is deferred. The credible-enemy precheck remains because removing it made the benchmark more expensive. This is useful consolidation, not a new fuel gate or a certified tighter physical bound.
* `want_home` sends Willow away from a visible hit or sufficiently probable unseen hit, except while holding/contesting capture. Station enemy safety uses the same predicate. Thus scattered low-probability unseen possibilities can still contribute conservative damage while failing the binary threat threshold; these are distinct outputs. Support exposure still rejects a live damaging camp member's reach **even asleep**, or a next-round respawn. The old Willow-specific sleeping-camp escape ordering/bailout is removed, not every sleeping-camp precaution.
* `willow_economy_or_escape` and the post-goal re-entry disappear. Ordinary retreat handles flight. `support_work` protects capture, bankable bags and shopping from formation/search interruptions; Willow heals first there, and a reachable strike can still occur later. This is relevant to conversion, without proving a new safe bank in Fracture or a saved carrier on Starvation.
* Local Prism recovery, dated mailbox requests/offers, four-turn recovery/formation bounds, stationary productive support, independent one-step heals and no-progress banking heals remain. The independent step still requires gain greater than three basics and protects immediate camp finishes. Productive actions/route progress can still reset formation duration. This is not a general support-job economic optimizer.
* `heal_ready`/`heal_target` share readiness/eligibility. `worth_healing` retains60%-of-cap or below50%HP acceptance. Combat search now caps heal utility at `min(actualGain,8*damage_bound(allyPosition))`, preventing a safe ally's restoration from always displacing a reset. This is the caller's Safety model evaluated at the ally's tile, not a native forecast of that recipient's exact damage. The cap is search-specific: ordinary `heal_ally` still uses absorbed gain. It can reduce search priority for quiet recovery and does not calculate actual recall savings. More Focus can still raise the acceptance floor and suppress some heals.
* Successful support walks use `see_revealed`; failed movement still does full `see_again` for hidden-blocker state. A successful turn-end ward triggers the support-offer recheck; otherwise it avoids the repeated full observation. This changes the observation/invalidation seam and needs current-source witnesses for future instrumentation.
* CHANGES111 caches a visible ally's pending stun with its stats, avoiding a later native read after a move hides it. CHANGES112 allows a longer retreat step only if its full damage bound is zero and no nonlengthening candidate is zero. The latter is a different mechanism from choosing a nonzero but surviving camp escape.

## Consequences for queued ideas and the incoming cases

**Focus/service utility:** `main_stat`, `Upkeep`, `upkeep_cost`, `best_emblem`, actual-prefix quotes and their cache are unchanged from canonical4db. No fixed first Bruiser Focus or service-aware shopping is included. The new helper and search cap make a future cap-demand diagnostic cleaner but also change which opportunities must be counted. Preserve the post-Focus design as a hypothesis; rebase a single exact fixed-Focus arm onto Astraeus and verify purchase activation before extending it. Do not carry over eight-cell healing totals as validation for the new support schedule. Fracture's lost Willow bags and Starvation's large healing total remain reasons to measure access/conversion rather than nominal healing capacity; Expanse remains a productive independent-Willow control.

**Camp survival:** `flee_camps` remains the same exit-burden minimizer without a lethal-versus-surviving partition. Farm806's nominated84-damage step is not the new zero-bound sideways escape. The primary survival-first and separate basic-kill emergency contracts still address a source gap. Their old runtime outcomes cannot transfer automatically, particularly because Astraeus now reaches ordinary retreat differently. Reuse the current bound/member semantics and qualify each exact port; do not restore the removed Willow-wide escape block wholesale.

**Band and make-way:** Astraeus `pathing.cpp` is byte-identical to local4db. The observed live-route/cached-approach disagreement is not repaired by the Willow refactor, and the03aaf optional make-way exception handling is absent. Keep the qualified Band mask diagnostic on its original exact source until causality is resolved; a new source needs its own prefix qualification. CHANGES111's stun-read fix addresses a different read site and is not proof the make-way failure is gone.

**Ward work:** the own-ward viewer exception and its native lookup in `Search::viewers` are unchanged; the plan diff is heal weighting/guard handling. A demand-driven exact ward fast path remains plausible, but the new turn-end ward/observation optimization changes profiling opportunities. Preserve personal-owner visibility, immediate placement and ordinary versus ward viewer results; benchmark the exact candidate source before combining it with policy.

**Allocation:** mixed-map nonsealing Monolith admission still rejects live bosses, and siege readiness still assigns them zero clear-round budget. Astraeus does not implement phased cleanup or Claudus's solo heal-trip boss job. Gold Rush's exact three-pick mirror has no Willow at all; a Willow-only refactor cannot by itself explain away that economic snowball. General111/112 changes can alter it, but no fresh actor-known mechanism has been shown.

The next reviewable comparison is a frozen Astraeus source baseline with correct own drafts and independent exact ports, after9b audits close. Keep fixed Focus, make-way robustness, survival and any exact ward optimization separate long enough to verify activation and native/fuel behavior. A lower global OOF count cannot establish that a particular lost job or actor death was fixed; conversely, failure to activate a mechanism is not a competitive rejection of it.
