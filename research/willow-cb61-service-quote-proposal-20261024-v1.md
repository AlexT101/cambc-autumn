**Implement one quote-only marginal-service observer; do not yet change purchases.** It should compare two actual affordable/completed builds against the same observed successful healing opportunities, including lost heals from the 60% threshold. Existing evidence is insufficient to establish a positive current-cb61 service-ranking witness. A blanket Focus/Health preference or fixed gold concentration on Vesper is not justified.

## What changed, and what did not

Cb61 fixes current-tile/own-ward heal visibility, permits Prism to recognize an ally's forthcoming ready heal despite its trailing recall stub, heals after a support step reveals danger, and stops dead camp members from vetoing an independent heal step. It also prices Prism recovery using the actual spawn route. These changes can increase **reachable, correctly scheduled service**, not heal capacity. Native self `can_cast`, cooldown, range, committed-recall exclusion, and the existing target rule still apply. Prior missed-opportunity rates and item experiment healing totals cannot be transplanted onto this source.

The canonical chooser still lacks the earlier fixed first-Bruiser-Focus override. `bot.cpp:120` selects Willow's main stat as Strength because she has no damaging Focus-scaled spell; `raw_output` and `upkeep_cost:1211` model her own clearing and recovery. Health/mitigation already have value through usable health and injury trips. Healing has no separate item-service value. Thus Strength is the model's present preference, not proof that maximizing personal damage is the correct support policy. The earlier document's “after the fixed first Focus” premise is conditional and does not describe cb61 inventory policy.

Completed evidence argues for measurement. The four selected Focus cells had common-horizon healing changes of approximately +1598, −1509, +406 and +183 HP, with completed recalls 8→7,16→18,9→9,11→10. The later 1104-game Focus-plus-make-way compound finished518–586 against its parent's524–580 on the same panel: no overall gain, and the compound does not isolate Focus. Health's Corners comparison delivered7737 versus12285.5 healing with12 versus8 completed recalls; Health+Focus's002-B win instead had greater team capital and healing despite much less Willow income, while002-A regressed. Neither personal income nor total heal HP alone identifies the better allocation. This is an observation selector, not a purchase override. Begun items remain committed. No first-Focus assumption, new movement policy, or new shared-memory protocol is needed. If the selector never activates, report no witness rather than broadening the gameplay policy.

Repeated helper calls must not count the same successful cast twice. This is bounded extra arithmetic, not a claim of zero fuel cost; debug logging must follow the repository's tracing rules and cannot establish fuel-exact release branches.

## Threshold and farm-cost accounting

`worth_healing` requires positive gain and either gain≥60% of cap or recipient HP fraction<50%. Therefore evaluate acceptance separately for each cap. Example: caps300 and360 with deficit190 at≥50% health give190 versus **zero**, not +60. For a completed Bruiser comparison relative to today's capH, Strength adds15 heal capacity and Focus adds75: compare `accepted(D,H+15)` against `accepted(D,H+75)`. The maximum same-target advantage is60, and a deficit already filled byH+15 gives no gain. The higher cap raises the ordinary threshold36 HP. Apply exact prefix stats to the affordable version.

Keep the full12-Strength loss of the completed Focus alternative in the existing `base−upkeep_cost(candidate)` term; both completed Bruiser alternatives add50HP. Extra healing does not enter `raw_output`, camp admission, or safety. On the fixed successful-cast schedule, a bigger heal consumes no additional cast or movement; the existing heal already displaced any competing reset. When the alternative suppresses a heal, do not automatically credit a reset that was never chosen. A changed target/schedule, additional detour, or heal newly replacing farm work needs separate evidence before pricing. Health receives no direct service HP because Willow cannot self-heal; its existing own-recovery benefit remains intact.

If this observer produces a signed positive service margin large enough to reverse the actual own-upkeep preference on cb61, a later isolated chooser experiment can use one shared full/prefix utility and a profile revision in the `prices` key. That later cache/interface work is necessary; merely returning a new item with the old savings quote would change farm/shop decisions inconsistently. This note authorizes neither that policy change nor the observer's execution.

## Falsifiable mechanism and allocation decision

First ask whether cap-binding **accepted same-target demand** remains after cb61's access fixes, whether suppressed heals cancel its uplift, and whether the affordable-prefix comparison—not only the completed emblem—reverses the cost ranking. Report eligible-cast coverage, unknown/planner share and actual purchase activation. Stop if no current-source ranking witness exists.

In any later isolated policy test, compare completed recipient recalls and resumed productive actions/captures after heals; Willow camp damage, own captures and completed tributes; actual target distribution, deaths, team collected/submitted gems; and fuel/failed actions. Higher HP delivered without more recipient work or with larger lost Willow production falsifies the proposed conversion. A profitable local service quote is still not a proof of a full-panel gain.

For gold allocation, the implementable next question is whether Willow's next purchase buys enough **observed marginal service** to compensate for its own lost farming—not whether all income should always go to one champion. Current quotes are personal, unshared and uncalibrated for ally service, so comparing Willow's raw income to Vesper's cannot settle that question. This proposal supplies one missing measurement without forcing either champion to abandon available productive captures.
