## A narrow, testable source explanation

Frozen `bot.cpp:4154–4192` computes `want_home` before deciding whether to keep a recall. Its general home keep needs an unthreatened square, no immediate safe tribute and `recall_outruns`. When holding a capture, it then tries `capture_recall`; ordinary shop/bank keeps are in the **else** branch. A false capture keep falls through to cancellation even if no productive action follows.

`capture_recall:3340–3350` rejects a channel that lands before estimated payout. Its special Prism retained-gem branch (`3352–3372`) additionally prices a heal/shop trip. `paying_soon:2810–2818` uses payout within two rounds and nearest recent enemy farther than three; that result suppresses `outmatched` and pursuit in `want_home:2820,2854–2869`. Private payout comes from `held_since + capture_time +1` (`world.h:358–361`), not directly from this exporter.

If Prism's estimate is the observed payout740, a coherent explanation is:737 keeps a home recall while payout is more than two rounds away;738 makes `paying_soon` true, which suppresses pack/pursuit pressure and changes `home` to false; yet the one-turn channel would land739, so `capture_recall` refuses it for leaving before payout. The combination can revoke an already-progressed safe exit precisely when the small capture gets close. The738 nearest distance6, unchangedHP and no useful action after cancellation fit this explanation. **The private home/payout/stand outputs are not recorded, so this branch remains a hypothesis.** A different private payout estimate or threat result would falsify it.

There is no demonstrated double-counted capture price: the early-payout test occurs before the economic expression, and no recorded internal quote shows a duplicate reward. Nor is the lesson that all Prism bags should bank immediately; retaining their Focus is an intentional productive policy. Here completion would first heal/shop, and its later banking behavior remains a separate decision.

## Decisive-capture counterexample and bounded decision fixtures

`game_ender` (`bot.cpp:3424–3446`) concerns a **visible enemy** whose carried gems win and which this actor can intercept. It does not protect our own capture from abandonment. `holding_capture` (`1494–1515`) rejects an already-winning bag and checks round-limit banking feasibility, but permits a capture that supplies the missing winning gems. The private early-arrival condition does not distinguish that valuable hold from an ordinary one. Therefore the existing game-ender check plus payout timing is **insufficient** for the proposed exception.

Reuse the existing `capture_recall` classification (`3350`): decline only the **new** contextual keep when `capture_share(world, g) >= gems_short(world)`. The share calculation uses current visible allied holders and the existing remainder rule (`1446–1452`); `gems_short` includes own bag and visible allies' ongoing tributes (`436–442`). This adds small bounded allied scans, not a path field, native observation or fight simulation. It does not prove that waiting wins, account for unknown future allies, or force the actor to hold dangerously: it leaves canonical cancellation/dispatch to choose. Do not apply this exclusion to the unchanged normal `home=true` keep, which already may retreat from a winning capture for safety.
