# Willow Health selection and first protection

2026-10-23. Source and saved evidence only, against canonical a67 and first-protection arm 2ae444fa. No new test, extraction, script implementation or gameplay edit during this review.

Health is allowed, but the objective structurally favors Strength before observed injury. Willow's `main_stat` is always Strength because Heal is not a damaging cast (`bot.cpp:113–124`). Her fresh choices are Power/Jungle/Bruiser Strength, Durability/Bruiser Health, plus Jungle Health before her first Jungle (`1277–1285`). Power Health is absent. Power Strength starts as the winner and ties use strict greater-than, retaining earlier candidates. Health adds no Strength through a passive for Willow.

`value` (`1240–1249`) builds the actual remaining shards and completion bonus, then compares own upkeep. Health enlarges the usable-health denominator, `0.65 * max_health`; Jungle and Durability additionally reduce future modeled damage. Strength shortens modeled clearing and proportionally reduces camp damage while clearing. `upkeep_cost` includes a positive clearing term `(camp_turns + 1)/(turns + 1)`, even before a recorded hit. Pure Health saves zero in this objective when observed incoming damage is zero, while Strength can still save clearing time. A Monolith-only synthetic upkeep estimate is the exception. This is a systematic early preference, not proof that Health always loses: sufficiently high injury and long recovery travel can make Health win.

The model omits discrete burst survival, healer service uptime, recipient continuation and the value of avoiding death downtime. Its injury input is net self-HP loss between recorded turns, partitioned using `Safety::camp_damage` (`1116–1126`, `5315`), not a gross damage counter. The model therefore need not represent exposure hidden by intervening recovery. No coefficient currently translates extra Health into extra actual heals. Adding healing to raw damage would corrupt camp and crew estimates.

Partials and quotes matter. Existing begun items return before fresh comparison, including a Willow Jungle Health partial. The purchase loop updates a `Build bought` with actual shards/completion stats. Fresh candidates are compared by total modeled saved time for completion; quotes use remaining cost. Treasure normally overrides the fresh winner, inherits its stat, and advertises the delayed winner's `saved`, rather than recomputing Treasure's own saving. `affordable_prefix` separately recomputes the actual purchased prefix, including mitigation only if it completes. Thus forced Treasure Health would inherit a pre-existing proxy-pricing ambiguity. Returning `value(jungle, health_stat)` directly avoids that new ambiguity. Existing `prices` still caches by share/stats/main and Prism home/monster ownership, not upkeep history: its cached choice can lag a fresh quote as injury history changes. That is a general baseline limitation, not a reason to bundle a cache rewrite into a Health experiment.

At Willow's initial 700 HP and 55 Strength, equal-cost Jungle choices are:

| Purchase | Jungle Strength | Jungle Health |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Each 30-gold shard | +3 Strength | +50 max HP |
| Completed 150-gold item | +15 Strength, first 30% camp mitigation | +250 max HP, same first 30% mitigation |
| Heal cap / ordinary worthwhile threshold | 360 / 216 HP | 360 / 216 HP |

Before completion, neither gives Jungle mitigation. Full Health raises the model's usable HP from 455 to 617.5; full Strength gives a clearing ratio of 55/70. In normalized initial-state terms, if `C` is clearing cost and `D/P` are baseline camp/champion recovery costs, Strength costs `(55/70)C + 0.55D + P`; Health costs `C + (49/95)D + (14/19)P`. Their winner depends on actual pressure and travel. This is model arithmetic, not a survival or healing outcome.

Health bought on spawn also heals by the gained amount, per the rules; raising current and maximum HP together preserves the prior missing-HP gap. Spawn also heals fully at turn end. The quote does not explicitly price this immediate current-HP effect. Willow cannot heal herself. Her own Health does not raise her outgoing heal cap or reduce ally overheal at an unchanged recipient deficit. Unlike added Focus, Jungle Health does not raise the 60%-of-heal worthwhile threshold (`self_abilities.cpp:419–420`). Bruiser Health and Bruiser Strength both add the same one Focus shard at completion, so their heal cap/threshold also match each other. Extra uptime could change delivered healing, but stats alone do not establish it.

Saved exact-a67 BigWorld/Boss A witnesses have Willow max HP 700, with Treasure Strength followed by Jungle Strength; Boss later adds Power Strength. Both have zero deaths, despite 3160/2960 camp damage taken. This neither proves enough HP nor demonstrates a Health benefit. The historical generic Health tests excluded Willow and were level/negative; they are not this intervention (`EXPERIMENTS.md:138–145`).

The saved six-smoke summary provides a useful narrower control: 2ae's actual first-protection activations in BigWorld and Corners are Jungle Strength. BigWorld later buys one Treasure Health shard; Expanse later completes Durability Health but does not activate the first-protection override. Thus the arm has bought some Health, yet has not tested its first protective item in Health. No qualifying purchase is not proof of a policy no-op: `best_emblem` also feeds prices and return/shop quotes.

The smallest separate Health arm is therefore a sibling of frozen 2ae: only inside its existing fresh first-protection override, replace a selected Jungle Strength with `value(jungle, health_stat)`. Keep all partials, Treasure-partial exclusion, either-protection once-only gate, Durability winner, and resumption of Treasure. Compare directly with 2ae to hold family/timing policy constant. Quotes must retain actual Health savings, even if small or zero. Measure purchase/quote activation, attacks, captures/capital, recalls, deaths, actual healing and fuel together; do not infer improvement from the extra HP or a named-map win.
