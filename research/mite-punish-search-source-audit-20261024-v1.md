# Visible vulnerability: bounded latest-Astraeus source audit

2026-10-24. Source-only review of frozen `bots/baselines/astraeus-4d08241/src` (aggregate `f599a31faa0251321beb97a726918286e266218ceb3e183b8271e41c5c84718c`). No build, simulation, decoder or gameplay changes. Deliberate Willow-first ladder batches are excluded.

**A generic “punish spent dash / low HP” bonus would duplicate existing mechanisms.** Current search reads actual visible target HP/shield, prices a legal kill and its carried gems, and removes the killed target from retaliation (`plan.cpp:111–116,1095–1128,1195–1242`). Already-received camp damage therefore makes a target easier and more valuable to finish without a special camp-victim rule. Enemy strike construction uses observed ready rounds, including dash/reset exclusivity and ranged movement spending the basic (`safety.cpp:719–758,778–796`). The one-/two-activation threat horizon uses acted state and team ordering (`804–811,837–861`); search removes a stunned enemy's retaliation only when the stun eliminates its sole activation before ours (`plan.cpp:1127–1128`). Chase also reprices our actual cast cooldowns and delayed allies (`safety.cpp:2216–2246`). These are legal local-combat estimates, not a guarantee that every profitable pursuit is found.

The two observed Mite windows do not establish an Astraeus missed kill. Evidence is `research/mite-observable-counter-review-20261024-v1.md` and the following files under `provenance/ladder-history-replays-20261024-v1/`:

- `actor-window-exports-v1/big-world-new-team4-loss-ryth2.json`, rounds165–174: Ryth waits through repeated140 camp hits, then dies during his exit. No enemy is visible to him. This supports an escape-budget defect in that actor, not proof our champion saw or could reach him.
- `actor-window-exports-v1/corridors-procless-loss-vendetta6.json`, rounds13–22, plus `actor-window-selected-step-v1/corridors-vendetta6-r18-step3189.json`: after dashing, Vendetta has spent the cast and sees nearby ready enemies before walking closer. That exposes him to a legal walk/reset alternative. The snapshot is **his view**, not the receiving opponent's view, and the opponent is not source-attributed Astraeus. Initial dash versus subsequent advance must remain distinct.

**One concrete conservative omission exists: future neutral damage to the enemy is not part of the combat valuation.** `Safety::stand` and `turns_to_kill_idle` populate only `us.camp` (`safety.cpp:2293–2331`); fight/chase construction supplies no enemy camp damage. The combat simulator applies that charge to us on enemy activations (`568–576`, with equivalent quiet-path accounting), rather than simulating the next actual neutral phase symmetrically. Search likewise charges our endpoint camp exposure (`plan.cpp:1727–1736,1794`), while target health decreases through our simulated hits. Thus a legal nonlethal hit or stun that would leave a visible enemy dying to an unavoidable next camp phase can be undervalued. This is a source omission, **not an identified missed opportunity in these windows**.

No combat patch is justified by this sample alone. In particular, raw camp range is not guaranteed enemy damage: their remaining activation/mobility, camp stun/sleep, changed closest-target aggro and ties after our movement can invalidate it. If a later source-known receiving-actor trace exposes this case, the smallest useful comparison is one candidate action whose resulting visible enemy cannot act again before the neutral phase, with the actual awake camp and resulting aggro known. Compare the current leaf value with that single phase's independently established damage; do not add a global aggression bonus or assume “dash spent” means no legal escape. Objective conversion remains the stronger demonstrated counter opportunity here.
